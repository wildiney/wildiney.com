A survey of 147 design system professionals asked what they measure. 18 metrics on the list. Adoption leads at 41%. ROI comes last at 5%.

The survey is by zeroheight, which sells documentation tooling for design systems, in its fifth edition. 56% track the system through analytics built into Figma, with no additional tool required, and only 9% have a dedicated tool to track code adoption. ROI does not come out of any ready-made analytics tool. It requires custom analysis, and that is why it ends up at the bottom of the list.

Lack of resources and headcount is the biggest challenge cited by the field, at 56%, far ahead of anything else on the list. Teams that do not measure ROI have no number to back a request for more budget or more people.

The same pattern appears in how these teams use AI. 71% already generate code with the tool, 60% generate documentation. Design generation sits at 24%, and it is the step that concerns the field most: 61% name it as the biggest risk, compared to 35% for code.

What the team measures and what the team delegates to AI follow the same logic: what is cheap and safe to do comes first, what requires more judgment comes later. The reason: a wrong component replicates across every screen that inherits that token, carrying the error through the entire product. Wrong code or documentation can be fixed with a single targeted change.

The report summarizes it plainly: "as a sector, we have not yet figured out what to measure." Another number confirms this: 91% report high or moderate confidence in their design system, but only 7% report full adoption across all teams in the company. Confidence alone does not solve the problem of proving value.

Having a complete component library, with no gap that forces the team to recreate a component by hand, was the most cited reason among successful teams: 79% named it as the primary reason for adoption, compared to 59% for communication and 25% for automation.

Before the next design system update, it is worth picking one of the 18 metrics that is already capturable through analytics and bringing that number to the next conversation about priorities. Be careful not to pick only vanity metrics.
